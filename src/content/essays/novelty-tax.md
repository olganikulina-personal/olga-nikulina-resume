Every new framework, every new tool, every new pattern, every new abstraction has a sticker price and a tax. The sticker price is what you read in the README. The tax is everything else.

[placeholder body — replace with the real essay]

The tax compounds. A new framework means a new mental model for the people who didn't pick it; new failure modes for the on-call rotation; new edge cases the docs don't cover; new debates about which file goes where; new "how do we do X here, again?" questions in the team chat. None of it shows up in the bake-off.

I am not anti-new. New is how you find out what's possible. New is how you don't atrophy.

But I have learned to ask a specific question before adopting a new thing: *what does this cost the team for the year after we pick it?* Not the demo cost. Not the migration cost. The boring, daily, six-months-from-now cost of *this is how we do things here, now*.
